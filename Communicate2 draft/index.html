FAQ’s

Common questions about our speech pathology services
What is a speech pathologist?
A speech pathologist is a clinically trained health professional who assesses, diagnoses and treats communication and swallowing issues for all ages.
What is the difference between a speech pathologist and a speech therapist?
Both terms are used, but a speech pathologist is a university-qualified practitioner often with clinical credentials and broader scope.
How do I know if my child needs a speech pathologist?
If language growth is behind peers, your child avoids talking, or has difficulty with reading or social communication, assessment is recommended.
What are speech milestones for a child’s age?
Typical milestone guidance is: 12 months first words; 2 years word combinations; 4 years clear speech for strangers. Individual variation is normal.
How much does speech therapy cost?
Costs depend on service type (assessment, therapy, diagnosis). We provide a clear quote after a free initial consult call.
Do you bulk bill or accept Medicare?
We offer private billing. Some clients use NDIS or private health rebates when eligible.
Can I use NDIS funding for speech pathology?
Yes, we support NDIS participants with funded plans and report-writing on request.
