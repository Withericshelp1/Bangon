Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this commercial finance calculator?
The calculator provides indicative repayment estimates based on the figures you enter. Commercial lending is more nuanced than residential — the rate you're offered will depend on the property type, your business financials, serviceability, loan-to-value ratio and the lender's current appetite. Use this as a starting point, then speak to us for a personalised assessment.
What deposit do I need for a commercial property loan?
Commercial property loans typically require a deposit of 20–35%, depending on the property type, location, tenancy profile and lender. Owner-occupied commercial properties (where you operate your business from the premises) generally attract more favourable LVR conditions than investment commercial properties. Some lenders will consider additional security to reduce the deposit required.
What is the difference between P&I and interest-only repayments?
With Principal & Interest (P&I), each repayment reduces your loan balance as well as covering the interest charge. With Interest Only, your repayments cover just the interest for a set period — your loan balance does not reduce during this time. Interest-only periods are common in commercial lending and investment structures, but the P&I repayments that follow are higher as the same principal must be repaid over a shorter remaining term.
How long does commercial finance approval take?
Commercial property finance typically takes 4–8 weeks from application to settlement, depending on the complexity of the deal, the lender's assessment requirements and whether valuations are required. Equipment finance and unsecured business loans can often be approved within 24–72 hours. We'll give you a realistic timeline at the outset and keep you informed throughout.
Can Bang On help with refinancing existing commercial debt?
Yes. Refinancing commercial property or business debt is one of the most effective ways to reduce your interest costs, free up cash flow or restructure your repayments. We review your existing arrangements, compare the market and identify whether a switch makes financial sense — factoring in any break costs, establishment fees or LVR changes that may apply.
