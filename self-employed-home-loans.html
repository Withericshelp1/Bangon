Self-Employed Lending

Self-Employed Does Not Mean Unfinanceable

Self-employed borrowers often face more scrutiny than salaried applicants. Lenders cannot simply look at a payslip so they dig deeper into your financials. This can feel overwhelming, especially if you have already been knocked back by a bank.

At Bang On Investments, we specialise in self-employed home loans. We understand the difference between low doc, full doc and alt doc lending and we know which lenders are most flexible for different income structures. Whether you are a sole trader, company director, contractor or run a trust, we have helped borrowers in your situation before.

Under the ATO's definition, you are self-employed if you operate as a sole trader, in a partnership, or as a director of your own company. Lenders treat each structure differently. Sole traders typically need to show two years of personal tax returns, while company directors may need to demonstrate their business income separately. We understand these distinctions and know which lenders assess each structure most favourably.

We work with self-employed clients across Sydney including Chatswood, Strathfield, Burwood, Concord, North Sydney, Bowral and the Southern Highlands.

Loan Types

Self-Employed Loan Options We Work With

Full Doc Loans

If your business has been operating for two or more years and you have current tax returns and financial statements, a full doc loan typically offers the best rates. We help you present these documents in the strongest possible way. Many self-employed clients qualify for better terms than they expect.

Find out if you qualify. Call 0414 406 887

Low Doc Loans

For borrowers who cannot provide standard income documentation, low doc loans use alternative evidence including 12 months BAS statements, an accountant's letter or business bank statements. We work with lenders who offer competitive low doc products for self-employed borrowers.

Want to know if low doc suits you? Call 0414 406 887

Alt Doc and Specialist Lending

For borrowers with complex income structures such as multiple companies, trusts or fluctuating income, specialist and non-bank lenders can offer more flexible assessment criteria. We know which lenders work best for complex self-employed situations.
